# Forage indicators for the climate toolkit

For [issue #92](https://github.com/CGIAR-Climate-Data-Hub/climate-toolkit/issues/92), the most useful first output is a **season-to-date chart for a grazing area**. Show the observed NDVI curve against its usual seasonal range and, where available, accumulated dry-matter productivity (DMP) against the same elapsed portion of past seasons. A separate historical view can answer whether typical conditions have changed. These are proposed outputs. The [pastoral reporting design](issue_92_pastoral_context_design.md) describes their place alongside weather and the [architecture summary](../package_architecture_summary.md) describes current capabilities.

## What the report should tell a user

| User question | Proposed calculation and display | Interpretation to print beside it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How green is the grazing area for this date? | Show NDVI, historical median and 10th–90th percentile band for the same calendar composite and polygon. Report the difference in index units, percentile rank, reference years, number of valid years, usable area and last observation date. Let the user inspect EVI from the same acquisition. | NDVI follows green vegetation. EVI can help in dense vegetation where NDVI saturates. Both respond to woody vegetation as well as grass. They share imagery, so agreement gives limited independent evidence. [NASA index guidance](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/measuring-vegetation-ndvi-evi/) |
| Has the season produced less dry matter than usual? | Multiply each valid DMP rate (kg dry matter/ha/day) by its actual number of days in the window. Sum to kg dry matter/ha and compare with historical totals through the **same completed interval**, including short or long month-end intervals. Show difference, valid days and any provisional values. | A low total suggests less vegetation growth. Recent grazing, trampling, species composition and access determine how much a herd can eat. Use partial-period estimates only with explicit coverage. [Copernicus DMP](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/products/vegetation/dry-matter-productivity-v2-0-300m) |
| Has this grazing season changed over decades? | Plot one value per comparable season and year, e.g. median NDVI or accumulated DMP. Estimate a Theil–Sen slope per decade and an uncertainty interval that accounts for temporal dependence. Show the years, sample count and gaps. Check sensitivity to endpoints, reference period, land-cover changes and sensor transitions. | A rise in greenness may reflect more woody cover. Inspect grassland cover, land use and rainfall history before attributing a trend to pasture recovery or management. Short records can still support a seasonal comparison. [Rangeland greening study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geosus.2020.12.002) |
| Does low greenness mean feed is gone or nutritionally poor? | Pair dry-season field observations or calibrated shortwave-infrared estimates with NDVI. Keep biomass in kg dry matter/ha, palatability as an ordinal score, and protein, fibre and digestibility in their assay units. | Dry grass can persist after green leaves disappear. Confirmation of a feed shortage needs standing forage, accessible area and herd demand. Local assays support nutritional claims. [Senegal dry-season field study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fenvs.2022.931299) · [Kenyan pasture assays](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30056650/) |

**Comparison policy.** Fix product, collection, polygon, land-cover definition and season for each comparison. Area-weight valid pixels. Compare like spatial support across years and flag a changing valid footprint. Exclude the target year from its baseline. Show baseline start/end and year count for each interval. Offer a common baseline for side-by-side products, e.g. complete overlapping years from 2014 onward, and a longer product-specific baseline where available. Label short or sparse baselines. Suppress percentile bands or trend estimates when coverage cannot support them. Use raw index differences in sparse rangelands where a percentage change from near-zero NDVI is unstable. During a season with data gaps, compare equivalent observed intervals or withhold the cumulative anomaly. Give separate estimates for locally distinct rainy seasons. FAO and USGS provide operational examples of comparing vegetation to the same period in earlier years. [FAO indicator definitions](https://eo.apps.fao.org/asis/index_2.jsp?lang=en&type=12) · [USGS anomaly method](https://earlywarning.usgs.gov/fews/product/903/)

## Product comparison

Reviewed 12 November 2026. The shortlist covers global historical records, East and West African monitoring products, and field evidence for forage quantity and nutritional quality. Coverage below describes the named collection. An ongoing archive can have local gaps and delayed publication. A dekad is one of three calendar intervals per month, starting on days 1, 11 and 21. Access observations refer to the route tested or documented.

| Product and footprint | Spatial resolution | Series and observation interval | Quality, access and best use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Terra MODIS MOD13Q1.061** NDVI/EVI, global land | 250 m | Feb 2000 onward, 16-day composites. About 26 years by 2026. | Best first greenness series. Use VI quality, pixel reliability and composite day. A cloud-free observation may come from anywhere in its interval. NASA catalogue returned a 16 Oct 2026 composite near Marsabit. Planetary Computer supported an anonymous range read on a historical Terra tile. Filter the Terra granule prefix MOD13Q1 when using a mirror that also serves Aqua. [NASA product](https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MOD13Q1.061) · [Mirror](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/api/stac/v1/collections/modis-13Q1-061) |
| **USGS FEWS NET eVIIRS NDVI**, African monitoring regions | 375 m | 2012 onward, overlapping 10-day composites updated every 5 days | Strong early-warning comparator. USGS smooths observations and revises recent periods. Use cloud flags and record whether values are interim. Consecutive updates share observations. Public USGS products include anomalies against a defined 10-year mean. [USGS product and method](https://earlywarning.usgs.gov/fews/product/903/) |
| **Copernicus NDVI v3**, global | 300 m | Jan 2014 onward, dekadal | Good cadence match to DMP. V3 reprocesses PROBA-V and Sentinel-3 with a consistent method and adjusts for viewing geometry. Retain version and flags around the sensor transition. [CLMS release](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/production-updates/version-3-0-of-normalised-difference-vegetation-index-products-in-nrt-operations) |
| **NOAA NDVI Climate Data Record**, global | 0.05° (about 5.6 km at equator) | 1981 onward, daily. About 45 years by 2026. | Longest regional comparison here. Screen quality before aggregating daily values. Mixed land covers within a cell limit pasture-scale interpretation. Test consistency across AVHRR and VIIRS. NOAA lists cloud and THREDDS access. [NOAA catalogue](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/climate-data-records/normalized-difference-vegetation-index) |
| **Copernicus DMP v2**, global | 300 m | Jan 2014 onward, dekadal growth rate. About 12 years by 2026. | Best first productivity candidate. Use final RT6 for retrospective comparisons and label near-real-time revisions. Historical reprocessing primarily supplies RT6. Public catalogue worked, file download via tested OData endpoint required credentials. [CLMS product](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/products/vegetation/dry-matter-productivity-v2-0-300m) · [Revision schedule](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/production-updates/version-2-0-of-dmp-gdmp-npp-and-gpp-300-m-in-nrt-operations) |
| **Historical Copernicus DMP v2**, global | 1 km | 1999–2020, dekadal, archive closed | Coarser historical comparison. Evaluate overlap with 300 m series, grids, sensors and biases before combining them. Same version number alone does not establish continuity. [CLMS archive](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/products/vegetation/dry-matter-productivity-v2-0-1km) |
| **FAO WaPOR v3** net primary productivity and actual evapotranspiration/interception | L1 global 300 m, L2 Africa and selected regions 100 m | 2018 onward, dekadal for these layers. About 8 years by 2026. | Useful water-use and carbon-productivity context. Inspect collection-specific quality, units, release stage and footprint. Shorter historical reference. FAO provides a portal and API. Polygon access performance remains untested. [FAO v3 coverage](https://www.fao.org/land-water/news/detail/WaPOR-v3-data-now-available-in-the-new-portal/) · [Layer details](https://www.fao.org/in-action/remote-sensing-for-water-productivity/news-and-events/news/news-detail/wapor-v3-data-now-available-on-the-google-earth-engine-data-catalogue/en) |
| **ACF/SIG Sahel annual biomass and total vegetation cover**, West Africa | Biomass 300 m as distributed, cover 500 m | Biomass 1999 onward, annual. Cover Jan 2001 onward, monthly from 8-day inputs. | Biomass in kg dry matter/ha shares Copernicus DMP inputs. Use for regional comparison. Cover includes green and dry material and can help interpret senescence. Check earlier input resolution before interpreting historical pixel detail. Raster and administrative products listed. [ACF data catalogue](https://sigsahel.info/en/download/) |
| **Global grassland aboveground biomass**, modelled annual stock | 0.005° (about 0.55 km at equator) | 2000–2022, 23 annual maps | Useful historical biomass comparison in g/m² (multiply by 10 for kg/ha). Authors report independent global RMSE of 93.69 g/m², about 937 kg/ha. Local performance needs separate evaluation. Annual maps cannot resolve dry-season depletion. Verified 23 GeoTIFF names in the August 2026 archive. The paper-linked deposit omits 2013. [Validation study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07544-1) · [Complete Zenodo release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21831183) |
| **Sentinel-2 optical reflectance**, land | 10 m red/NIR, 20 m red-edge and shortwave infrared | Mission from 2015, nominal 5-day equatorial revisit with two satellites. Clear scenes and surface-reflectance history vary by site and processing collection. | Best for small grazing areas and experimental dry-mass or quality models. Screen clouds, shadow and mixed pixels. Measure clear-observation frequency. Calibrate to local clips or assays. [ESA instrument](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-2/Facts_and_figures) · [Dry-forage field study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fenvs.2022.931299) |
| **Global Pasture Watch grassland extent v1**, global | 30 m | 2000–2022, annual | Candidate mask distinguishing cultivated and natural/semi-natural grassland probabilities. Check local omission of sparse pasture and browse, and disclose any use beyond 2022. [Producer methods](https://github.com/wri/global-pasture-watch/blob/main/ggc-30m/README.md) |
| **Global Pasture Watch grassland gross primary productivity**, global | 30 m | 2000–2022, one estimate every two months | Useful for historical carbon uptake on grassland. Its finer grid uses coarser weather inputs. Flux-tower validation assesses carbon flux. Local standing forage mass requires a separate calibration. [Producer study](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.19774) |
| **Rangeland Analysis Platform herbaceous production**, US rangelands | 30 m | 1986 onward, annual and 16-day production | Strong example of reporting new herbaceous growth by functional group. Latest in-season values are provisional. US coverage excludes African grazing areas. [Producer catalogue](https://rangelands.app/products) · [Resolution and methods](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/63366) |
| **JRC Global Surface Water**, global | 30 m | 1984–2024 archive, monthly history and long-term occurrence | Water context only. Use valid observation counts. Combining monthly history across releases requires attention to the Landsat collection change. Local functioning water points and access rights remain separate inputs. [JRC download and version notes](https://global-surface-water.appspot.com/download) |

| Reference | Spatial support | Series and sampling frequency | Useful comparison and limits |

| **Lower Nyando, Kenya pasture assays** | 75 herbaceous pasture samples across 20 villages and three zones | Four seasons in one year | Local reference for protein and fibre variability. Scope is western Kenya. [Original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30056650/) |
| **Brazilian tropical pasture study** | Georeferenced paddocks of an experimental Marandu grass pasture | January–April growing seasons in 2016–2020, field dates matched to scenes | Demonstrates how to test Sentinel-2 predictions of crude protein and fibre under known management. | **Kenya PLEWS forage condition index** | Kenyan counties in the published supplement | Three-month average associated with Jan 2017. Continuous open archive unverified. | A livestock-specific local comparator. Preserve its geographic aggregation and averaging period. The supplement has a CC BY 4.0 licence. [Authors’ data](https://doi.org/10.17632/h92t6y3pkm.1) |

**Implementation priority.** Pilot MODIS NDVI with optional EVI from the same asset collection, then add seasonal DMP where access and throughput are reproducible. Compare one wet and one dry period against a local reference. Validate missing-data handling and baseline sensitivity before exposing trend or percentile summaries. Keep annual biomass for historical context and Sentinel-2 plus field samples for a separate standing-forage and quality study. Neither a regional early-warning trigger nor a feed-quality score has been calibrated in this review.
